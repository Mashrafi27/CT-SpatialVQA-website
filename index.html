Abstract
Recent advances in 3D medical vision–language models have enabled joint reasoning over volumetric images and text, showing strong performance in medical visual question-answering (VQA) and report generation. Despite this progress, it remains unclear whether these models learn spatially grounded anatomy from 3D volumes or rely primarily on learned priors and language correlations.

To address this gap, we introduce CT-SpatialVQA, a benchmark designed to evaluate semantic–spatial reasoning in 3D CT data. The benchmark comprises 9,077 clinically grounded QA pairs derived from 1,601 radiology reports and CT volumes, validated via a robust LLM-assisted pipeline with a 95% human consensus agreement rate. Our dataset requires explicit anatomical localization, laterality awareness, structural comparison, and 3D inter-structure relational reasoning. We benchmark eight 3D medical VLMs, finding severe degradation on semantic-spatial reasoning, averaging 34% accuracy and often below random — highlighting the need for deeper integration of volumetric evidence for trustworthy clinical use.

Method & Dataset

Overview of CT-SpatialVQA. (top) The dataset generation pipeline uses 3D CT scans and paired radiology reports to prompt a “QA Generator LLM”, synthesizing candidate spatially-grounded QA pairs. An independent QA Validator LLM filters the candidates, followed by medical human auditors to produce the final benchmark. (bottom) The evaluation protocol tests each 3D medical VLM by inputting a 3D CT scan and a spatially-grounded question, with LLM-based evaluation and traditional text metrics assessing the output.

Dataset

CT-SpatialVQA is built on the CT-RATE dataset (test split) — 1,601 volumetric CT scans paired with board-certified radiology reports. Using GPT-4o as the Generator LLM and Gemini 2.5 Flash as the Validator LLM, we constructed 9,077 spatially grounded QA pairs averaging 5.67 pairs per report. The dataset is publicly available on HuggingFace.

Six Spatial Reasoning Categories

01

Laterality & Bilateral Symmetry

Left/right/bilateral grounding relative to the sagittal midline. Prevents laterality inversion and aids hallucination detection.

02

Longitudinal (Vertical) Position

Superior/inferior axis positioning. Requires volumetric integration along the cranio-caudal axis and slice-level consistency.

03

Anterior-Posterior (Depth) Relations

Front/back orientation across the axial stack. Preserves depth cues essential for compartment-level reasoning.

04

Medial-Lateral Orientation (Centricity)

Central vs. peripheral localization within anatomical reference frames across the full volume.

05

Adjacency & Containment

Topological relations: distinguishing structures that are touching from those contained within anatomical boundaries.

06

Spatial Extent & Boundaries

Tracks spatial continuity, confinement, and compartment crossing to model disease spread accurately.

Results

We benchmark eight state-of-the-art 3D medical VLMs in a zero-shot setting. LLM-as-Jury uses GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and Qwen3 as independent binary judges. All models fall below 50% accuracy, with the best model (CT-Chat) achieving only 43.69%.
